Cosmediate: marketplace audit and redesign

The three roles of the product were designed as if they were entirely different products, and I brought them into one design system.

Cosmediate is a Netherlands-based platform connecting patients with certified cosmetic clinics.

Client, Clinic and Admin had been built independently: a shared domain but no shared design system. The brief was broad: rebuild the whole thing so the experiences stay consistent while each role gets exactly what it needs.

Built three products on one logic and one style: for clients, clinics and admins. Solo, without a team.

One system for three roles

Components in the clinic dashboard have to stay consistent with the patient booking flow.

Before any screen work I mapped the information architecture per role, and then how they connect together.

How a clinic's availability surfaces in the client's booking flow, how admin actions affect what clients and clinics see. The design system was built for exactly this: semantic tokens instead of raw values and every component designed with all its states, because clinics live in edge cases, meaning fully booked slots, pending approvals and expired certifications.

Client: a trust-first booking flow

Booking a cosmetic clinic is not a restaurant reservation: what is missing is trust, not slots.

I designed the flow as a linear, step-by-step process where each step answers one question: clinic, treatment, slot, details, confirmation.

There is a consultation layer and certification requirements that generic booking interfaces do not address. The clinic profile does the trust-building before the user ever reaches booking: certifications, before and after photos, practitioner profiles and verified reviews sit above the booking button.

Clinic: operational density

A clinic should not have to read text to see what needs attention.

I structured the interface around three self-contained areas: Calendar, Profile, Settings.

A clinic managing multiple practitioners, treatment types and a high booking volume has to see the state of every booking at a glance. Booking management is built on the states pending, confirmed, completed and cancelled, each with a distinct visual treatment and a clear set of actions.

Admin: a queue, not a dashboard

The admin side is an internal tool: density over decoration.

I designed clinic approvals, certification verification, review moderation and user management each as a queue the admin works through top to bottom.

What the project required

Three-role products are hard because every decision echoes in the other roles.

Keeping that coherence from scratch, without a product team, meant holding the full system in mind at every step.

A change in how clinics manage availability changes what patients see in booking. The design system held those links together.
