# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, design leads and founders at product companies, screening a Senior Product Designer / Design Engineer candidate. They arrive from LinkedIn or a CV, usually with a stack of other portfolios open, and give the page under a minute before deciding whether to read a case. Bilingual audience: EN for international roles, UA for the local market, served as parallel page sets.

## Product Purpose

Convert a skeptical reviewer into a conversation. The site has to prove one claim fast — this designer ships the product, not the mockup — through five case studies written problem → what I did → result, plus five self-built tools with public repos. Success is a reply on Telegram or LinkedIn, not time on page.

## Brand Personality

Plain, proof-driven, unpolished on purpose. Every claim carries a number or a link to running code. The visual voice is deliberately un-designed: the accent is `#0000EE`, the browser's own default link blue, and links keep hover underlines. The serif italic (Charis SIL) is the one ornament, used only to mark the words that carry the argument. Confident without decoration; the work is the design, the page just gets out of its way.

## Anti-references

- Agency-portfolio maximalism: full-bleed scroll-jacked case reveals, animated cursors, splash screens.
- The editorial-typographic lane (display serif + mono labels + ruled columns) — it would swallow the un-designed stance.
- Dribbble-shot presentation: mockups floating on gradients, devices at an angle, no outcome stated.
- Corporate SaaS neutrality: navy, safe grays, stock photography, hero-metric templates.

## Design Principles

1. **Proof over polish.** Every section states an outcome. A screen with no result line does not earn its space.
2. **The default is the statement.** Unstyled browser blue, plain underlines, system-plain layout — the restraint is deliberate, not unfinished. Do not "fix" it into a template.
3. **Typography carries the hierarchy.** One sans (Onest) plus one serif italic for emphasis. No color coding, no boxes, no icon scaffolding.
4. **Both languages are first-class.** EN and UA pages are duplicated files; every change lands in both or it is not done.
5. **Read time is the budget.** A hiring manager gives this under a minute. Nothing may cost a scroll it did not earn.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥ 4.5:1 against the `#fbfaf8` ground; large display text ≥ 3:1. Text sized in `rem` so browser zoom works; layout must survive 200% zoom. Motion is minimal by design — the one looping video in the LayoutLab mockup is decorative and muted; any future motion needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative.
